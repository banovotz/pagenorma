Open **Translation Analyses**, find your project and click **Open Analysis**. The workspace has three columns, aligned paragraph by paragraph:

| Column | Content |
|---|---|
| **Source** | The original paragraph. |
| **Translation** | Your translation of it. |
| **LLM Commentary** | AI notes on that pair, where there is something to say. |

Each paragraph has a number such as `#12`, so you can find the same place in all three columns.

## Search

Type in **Search source, translation or comments** to filter the paragraphs. Suggestions appear as you type, and pressing **Enter** runs the search. A counter shows how many paragraphs match and which page of results you are on.

## Move through the text

Use **Previous**, **Next** and the page numbers under the columns.

## Hide untranslated front matter

Source books often start with pages you do not translate, such as the copyright page. With **Hide untranslated front matter** ticked, which is the default, those paragraphs are hidden and a note says how many. Untick it to see them.

## Highlights

When a comment refers to a specific expression in your translation, that expression is highlighted in the Translation column. Hover over it to see why it is marked.

## View the glossary

Click **View Glossary** above the commentary column to open the [project glossary]({% link support/terminology/project-glossary.md %}).

## Partial analyses

If only some pages were analysed, a notice at the top of the view says so. See [Choose which pages to analyse]({% link support/analysis/choose-pages-to-analyse.md %}).
